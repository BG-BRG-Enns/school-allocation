# Categories and rules

With categories and rules, a registration makes sure everyone does something in particular, such as “at least one sport activity” or “in year 7, at least 200 minutes of careers guidance”.

## Categories

Categories apply to all of the school's registrations. You find them under **Administration → Categories** or with the **Categories** button on the registration page; the administration and the organisation keep them.

- **Add category** creates one. A name exists only once, whatever its capitalisation.
- **Rename** changes the name everywhere, in every registration.
- **Remove** is only offered for a category that no activity and no rule uses. The table shows how many do.

An activity can belong to no category, one or several. Teachers choose them in the activity's form ([Proposing an activity](../lehrkraefte/aktivitaet)), and you can change them for any activity. Once the allocation is published, an approved activity's categories stay as they are, because the allocation was calculated with them. The catalogue shows the categories with every activity, and students can filter by one.

## Rules

You enter the rules under **Change settings** ([Settings](./einstellungen)) until registration opens. After that they are fixed: students choose by them. **Add rule** creates one. A rule has:

- a **Category**;
- the **Years** it applies to and, if needed, **Only these classes**, such as “7A, 7B”; left empty, it applies to every class of those years;
- what it **Counts**: activities or minutes, meaning the credited minutes of the activities;
- **At least**: 1 to 20 activities or 1 to 10000 minutes.

A registration has at most 20 rules. Class activities count: if the whole class goes on a trip in the category, the rule may already be met.

## What the rules do

- **Places and demand** shows, for each rule and year, how much the students still need after their class activities and the most the category's places can bring. If that is not enough, the rule is marked; the category then needs more activities.
- With their **choices**, students see the rules and how far their choices reach. Their choices are complete only once they reach every rule, as far as the catalogue allows.
- The **allocation** meets the rules after the minimum minutes and before the choices ([Allocation](./zuteilung)). A rule is not a must: where it cannot be met, the calculation shows who misses it. If a person's choices are not enough, they also get a remaining place with no cost for a rule.
- **After publication**, a swap is refused if a rule would then no longer be met or would miss even more. The class teacher places those who miss a rule. You can override a rule when changing the allocation by hand ([After publication](./nach-der-veroeffentlichung)).
